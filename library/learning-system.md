## Core Strategies (Why It Works)

**1. Forced output under time pressure**
The teacher asks without pause — you must answer in 3–5 seconds. This trains your brain to skip the translate-then-speak loop and produce English directly.

**2. Spaced repetition by design**
Every new word is recycled across the next 7–10 lessons automatically. No flashcard app needed — the curriculum is the spaced repetition.

**3. Real-time correction**
Every grammar or pronunciation error is corrected on the spot, mid-sentence if necessary. You never rehearse the wrong form long enough to solidify it.

**4. Long answers required**
You are never allowed to answer "Yes" or "No" alone. You must always answer in a full sentence. This builds sentence-construction reflex.

**5. Dictation as integration**
Each lesson ends with dictation — it forces you to process the lesson's vocabulary through listening, phonology, and writing simultaneously.

## Daily Study Plan (Solo, with this knowledge base)

Without a live teacher, the drilling loop is broken. Here is the workaround:

**Morning (~20 min) — Input**
1. Open `html/book-N.html`, navigate to today's lesson
2. Read the vocabulary section aloud — look at the original PDF page for context
3. Read each Q&A pair: cover the answer, say it aloud, then check

**Afternoon (~15 min) — Production**
4. Drill mode: read only the questions, produce the answer without looking
5. Record yourself on phone — play back and listen for errors

**Evening (~10 min) — Search & Review**
6. Open `html/search.html`, search any word you forgot during the day
7. Click through to see it in context on the exact page

**Weekly rhythm**

| Day | Activity |
|---|---|
| Mon–Fri | 1 new lesson per day (vocab → Q&A drill → dictation) |
| Saturday | Revision — re-drill all 5 lessons' Q&A, no new material |
| Sunday | Rest or listen to English podcasts at your current CEFR level |

**Pace:** 5 lessons/week → all 191 lessons in ~38 weeks (~9 months)

**What is missing (and matters):**
- Audio — the method was designed around hearing and repeating
- A drilling partner / teacher — the fastest-response loop is critical
- Authentic output feedback (speaking to real people)

Workaround: supplement with a language exchange partner (iTalki, Tandem) who drills you using the Q&A pairs from the `.md` files.

## Score: 68 / 100 for Spoken English Effectiveness

| Dimension | Score | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Speaking output volume | 95 | You speak almost 100% of class time |
| Grammar systematization | 85 | Progressive, recycled, corrected |
| Vocabulary retention | 80 | Spaced repetition is built into the curriculum |
| Authentic fluency transfer | 45 | Q&A drilling ≠ real conversation; robotic feel in practice |
| Pronunciation development | 40 | Correction-only, no phonics or modeling |
| Listening development | 50 | Only the teacher's voice; no varied authentic input |
| Writing / reading | 30 | Dictation only; virtually no authentic reading |
| Motivation / sustainability | 55 | Highly repetitive; many students drop out |

**Why not higher:** The method optimizes for one specific skill — responding quickly to structured questions — and does it extremely well. But real spoken fluency also requires listening to varied accents, handling unpredictable topics, using idioms naturally, and reading widely. The Callan Method barely touches these.

**Why not lower:** It genuinely produces faster spoken output than almost any classroom method. Students who complete it consistently report that speaking feels automatic.

## CEFR Level if Fully Mastered

**Realistic ceiling: B2–C1 speaking**

The book claims C2 (CPE) at Stage 12. In practice:

| Completion | Realistic speaking level |
|---|---|
| Stages 1–4 (80h) | B1 — can hold basic conversations, pass KET |
| Stages 1–7 (160h) | B2 — FCE-ready, professional conversations, travel fluency |
| Stages 1–12 (320h) | C1 — fluent, accurate, comfortable with complex topics |
| Stages 1–12 + 6 months authentic input | C2 — reachable |

True C2 requires what this method does not provide: sustained authentic exposure — reading novels, watching unscripted native content, writing essays, debating. Callan gets you to C1 mechanically; the final step to C2 must come from the real world.
